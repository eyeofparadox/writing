A deliciously thorny lexical shard: "drunkening" as a participial descriptor evokes a kind of mythic descent, a slow unraveling of coherence. It's rare, but not without precedent in poetic or experimental prose. Let's scaffold this properly.

### Semantic Equivalents and Resonance Mapping

You're seeking a term that:

- Functions adjectivally or adverbially (like "withering")
- Implies **progressive transformation**, not just a static state
- Carries **emotional or mythic weight**, not clinical detachment

---

Here are some resonance-tiered candidates:

#### Direct Equivalents (rare or coined)

- **Drunkening** — your original term. It works poetically, especially in mythic or surreal prose. It mirrors "darkening," "sickening," "quickening."
- **Intoxifying** — rare, but plausible. Evokes a process of becoming intoxicated.
- **Besotteding** — archaic and clunky, but could be stylized.
- **Liquefying** — metaphorical, if the friend is losing coherence.
- **Staggering** — too physical, lacks internal descent.

#### Mythic/Poetic Alternatives

- **Unmooring** — suggests loss of anchor, drifting into intoxication.
- **Dimming** — metaphorical, like a soul dimming under alcohol.
- **Unraveling** — emotional and cognitive disintegration.
- **Swaying** — gentle, rhythmic descent.
- **Slurring** — auditory cue, but evocative.

#### Codex-style Neologisms

- **Spiriting** — as in "taken by spirits," double entendre.
- **Alewarding** — directional, like "sunward," but toward drink.
- **Fermenting** — metaphorical, internal chemical shift.
- **Distilling** — ironic inversion: becoming less refined.

### Suggested Usage

> "A rapidly drunkening friend" works in mythic or poetic prose. If you want to preserve the cadence but shift the register:

- "A rapidly unraveling friend"
- "A swiftly intoxifying companion"
- "A friend dimming into drink"
- "A soul spiriting away into stupor"
